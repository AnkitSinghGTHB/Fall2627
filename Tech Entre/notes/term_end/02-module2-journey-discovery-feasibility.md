## 1. Opportunity Identification

### 1.1 Idea vs Opportunity
* **Idea**: A thought, impression, or concept that may or may not have commercial viability (e.g., "smart shoes that order coffee").
* **Opportunity**: A favorable set of circumstances that creates a genuine need for a new product, service, or business. 
* **Four Essential Attributes of a True Business Opportunity**:
  1. **Attractive**: Substantial profit margins and return on investment.
  2. **Timely**: Arriving in the market window before saturation, when tech/readiness align.
  3. **Durable**: Able to sustain value over years, not just a passing fad.
  4. **Anchored in Value**: Solves a real, painful problem or adds clear utility.

### 1.2 The 4 Major Environmental Sources of Opportunities

| Environmental Source | Description | Concrete Startup Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **1. Technological Shifts** | Breakthroughs in hardware, cloud, AI, or biotech enabling new business models | **OpenAI / Perplexity** emerging from Large Language Model transformer architectures |
| **2. Demographic & Social Changes** | Shifts in population age, urbanization, dual-income households, wellness trends | **Lenskart / Cult.fit** catering to urban health, screen-time vision, and lifestyle demands |
| **3. Regulatory & Government Policies** | New laws, decarbonization mandates, subsidies, or open APIs created by state | **Ather Energy / BluSmart** driven by FAME-II EV subsidies and carbon reduction policies |
| **4. Market Inefficiencies & Gaps** | Fragmented supply chains, opaque pricing, or poor customer experience | **Zepto / Blinkit** solving fragmented grocery supply chains with 10-minute dark stores |

## 2. Customer Discovery Process (Steve Blank Framework)

Developed by **Steve Blank** in the Customer Development Model. The core tenet is **"There are no facts inside the building, so get outside!"**

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            THE CUSTOMER DISCOVERY CYCLE                          |
|                                                                                  |
|  [ Step 1: State Hypotheses ]                                                    |
|  Document assumptions regarding: Customer, Problem, Solution, Channel, Pricing   |
|         |                                                                        |
|         v                                                                        |
|  [ Step 2: Test Problem Hypotheses ]                                             |
|  Conduct 50-100 open-ended user interviews. Validate if the pain point is real!  |
|         |                                                                        |
|         v                                                                        |
|  [ Step 3: Test Solution Hypotheses ]                                            |
|  Present Low-Fidelity MVP / Wireframes. Test Willingness-to-Pay (WTP).          |
|         |                                                                        |
|         v                                                                        |
|  [ Step 4: Verify & Pivot or Proceed ]                                           |
|  If hypotheses validated ──▶ PROCEED TO CUSTOMER VALIDATION                      |
|  If assumptions fail    ──▶ PIVOT (Iterate product, market, or pricing model)    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 3. The 4 Pillars of a Comprehensive Feasibility Study

A **Feasibility Analysis** is the process of determining whether a business idea is viable. It is conducted **before** writing a formal business plan.

```
                             FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS
                                      │
         ┌───────────────┬────────────┴─────────────┬────────────────┐
         ▼               ▼                          ▼                ▼
   1. Product/      2. Industry/Target        3. Organizational    4. Financial
   Service          Market                     Feasibility         Feasibility
```

### Pillar 1: Product / Service Feasibility
* **Concept Test**: Presenting a 1-page concept statement (product description, target market, benefits, unique selling proposition) to 20 prospective customers and industry experts.
* **Usability & Demand Test**: Building a minimum viable prototype (MVP) to assess whether the solution reliably addresses the core pain point.

### Pillar 2: Industry and Target Market Feasibility
* **Industry Attractiveness**: Evaluated using **Porter's Five Forces** (Rivalry, Supplier Power, Buyer Power, Threat of Substitutes, Threat of New Entrants). Growing, fragmented, high-margin industries are favored.
* **Target Market Sizing**:
  * **TAM (Total Addressable Market)**: Entire annual global revenue opportunity if 100% market share was attained.
  * **SAM (Serviceable Available Market)**: The portion of TAM within reach of the startup’s geographic and regulatory scope.
  * **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**: The realistic market share captured within 1–3 years with available capital.

### Pillar 3: Organizational Feasibility
* **Management Prowess**: Assessing founder-market fit, relevant technical competencies, domain experience, and leadership track record.
* **Resource Sufficiency**: Verifying availability of office/lab facilities, intellectual property protections, supply chain partnerships, and key hire availability.

### Pillar 4: Financial Feasibility
* **Total Startup Cash Needed**: Capital required to build MVP, register legal entity, and reach cash-flow break-even.
* **Financial Attractiveness & Unit Economics**:
  * **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: Total marketing and sales spend divided by new customers acquired.
  * **LTV (Lifetime Value)**: Net profit generated over the entire lifespan of a customer relationship.
  * **Rule of Thumb**: $LTV \ge 3 \times CAC$ for a healthy scalable business model.

## 4. Willingness-to-Pay (WTP) & Customer Validation

Validating whether customers will pay money before manufacturing inventory:
1. **Smoke Testing (Pre-order Landing Pages)**: Setting up a landing page detailing features and pricing with a "Pre-order Now" or "Deposit ₹99" button. Conversion rate directly demonstrates demand.
2. **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter**: Survey questions determining 4 critical price points:
   * Too cheap (quality suspect)
   * Cheap (bargain)
   * Expensive (needs consideration)
   * Too expensive (will not buy)

## 5. Model Exam Questions & Answers

### Question 1 (12 Marks)
**What is a Feasibility Study? Describe the four major types of feasibility analysis an entrepreneur must conduct before launching a venture.**

**Answer Structure for 12/12**:
1. **Introduction**: Define feasibility analysis as the operational, market, and economic filter that prevents wasted venture capital.
2. **Detailed Breakdown of the 4 Pillars**:
   - *Pillar 1: Product/Service Feasibility*: Concept testing, prototype feedback, feature prioritization.
   - *Pillar 2: Industry & Target Market Feasibility*: Porter's 5 Forces, TAM-SAM-SOM calculation with numerical examples.
   - *Pillar 3: Organizational Feasibility*: Founder-market fit, human capital, resource sufficiency.
   - *Pillar 4: Financial Feasibility*: Total startup capital requirements, financial projections, CAC vs LTV ratio ($LTV/CAC \ge 3$).
3. **Synthesis Matrix**: Provide a summary table contrasting the objective, primary tool, and pass/fail criteria of each pillar.

### Question 2 (8 Marks)
**Explain the Customer Discovery process with a flowchart. How does Customer Discovery prevent startup failure?**

**Answer Structure for 8/8**:
1. **Definition & Flowchart**: Draw the 4-step cycle (Hypotheses ➔ Problem Test ➔ Solution Test ➔ Pivot/Proceed).
2. **Failure Prevention Mechanisms**:
   - Eliminates "Building something nobody wants" (the #1 reason startups fail per CB Insights).
   - Validates problem urgency before engineering costs are committed.
   - Identifies exact buyer personas and sales channels early, preventing misallocated marketing spend.
