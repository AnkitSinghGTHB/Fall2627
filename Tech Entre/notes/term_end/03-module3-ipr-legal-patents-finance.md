## 1. Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) Overview

### 1.1 What is IPR?
**Intellectual Property (IP)** represents intangible creations of the human mind—such as inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, symbols, names, and images used in commerce. **IPR** grants legal monopoly rights to the creator for a designated duration to encourage R&D investment and economic progress.

### 1.2 The Master Comparative Matrix of IPR Forms

| IPR Form | Subject Matter Protected | Validity Period (India) | Core Legal Criteria | Governing Law (India) | Real-World Example |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Patent** | Novel inventions, functional processes, machines, compositions of matter | **20 Years** (from filing date) | 1. Novelty<br>2. Inventive Step (Non-obvious)<br>3. Industrial Applicability | The Patents Act, 1970 | mRNA vaccine formulation (Moderna / Pfizer) |
| **Trademark** | Brand names, logos, slogans, distinctive shapes, trade dress | **10 Years** (Renewable indefinitely every 10 yrs) | Distinctiveness, Non-deceptive, Not generic | The Trade Marks Act, 1999 | Apple logo, "Just Do It" slogan, Coca-Cola script |
| **Copyright** | Original literary, artistic, musical, dramatic works, software code | **Lifetime of Author + 60 Years** | Originality of expression (Fixation in tangible medium) | The Copyright Act, 1957 | Python source code, books, Hollywood films |
| **Trade Secret** | Confidential business formulas, recipes, algorithms, vendor lists | **Indefinite** (As long as secret remains confidential) | Commercial value derived from secrecy; reasonable security measures taken | Common law / Indian Contract Act (Sec 27) | Coca-Cola formula, Google PageRank algorithm |
| **Design Patent** | Aesthetic external shape, surface pattern, ornamentation | **10 + 5 Years** (Max 15 Years) | Novelty in visual eye-appeal; non-functional | The Designs Act, 2000 | Distinctive curved chassis of iPhone |

## 2. Patent Infringement, Defense & Legal Safeguards

### 2.1 Types of Infringement
1. **Direct Infringement**: An unauthorized party manufactures, sells, or imports an invention that embodies every claim element of a valid patent.
2. **Infringement under the "Doctrine of Equivalents"**: Even if the competitor modifies minor components, if the competing device performs **substantially the same function in substantially the same way to achieve the same result**, infringement holds.
3. **Contributory / Indirect Infringement**: Supplying a key custom component with knowledge that it enables an infringing system.

### 2.2 Legal Remedies for IPR Infringement
1. **Cease-and-Desist Notice**: Formal legal warning demanding immediate cessation of infringing commercial activity.
2. **Preliminary / Interlocutory Injunction**: Court order immediately restraining manufacture/sale during the pendency of the trial to prevent irreparable market harm.
3. **Permanent Injunction**: Final court ban post-trial.
4. **Damages or Account of Profits**: The infringer must surrender all net profits earned from the infringing goods, or compensate the patent holder for lost licensing royalties.

### 2.3 Critical Protective Agreements
* **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**: Legally binding contract obligating parties to hold proprietary disclosures confidential.
* **Non-Compete & Non-Solicitation Clauses**: Prevents key engineers from joining immediate rivals or poaching employees for a defined period.
* **IP Assignment Agreement**: Mandates that all code, designs, and patents created by employees or contractors automatically transfer full legal ownership to the startup entity.

## 3. Sources of Venture Finance (Seed to Scale)

```
[ Bootstrapping / FFF ] ──▶ [ Angel Investors / Grants ] ──▶ [ Seed Round ] ──▶ [ Series A / B / C ]
  (Ideation & Prototype)      (MVP & Pilot Traction)           (Product-Market Fit)     (Scale & Expansion)
```

1. **Bootstrapping (Self-Funding)**: Founders rely on personal savings and early revenues (e.g., Zerodha never took external VC money).
2. **Friends, Family & Fools (FFF)**: Informal low-interest or patient early equity capital.
3. **Angel Investors**: High-Net-Worth Individuals (HNIs) who invest ₹10L–₹1Cr in exchange for 5–15% early equity, providing strategic mentorship.
4. **Venture Capital (VC)**: Professional institutional funds investing institutional capital across formal priced rounds:
   * **Seed Round ($250K–$1M)**: Developing product, hiring core team.
   * **Series A ($2M–$10M)**: Scaling proven product-market fit.
   * **Series B & C ($10M–$50M+)**: International expansion, market domination, acquisitions.
5. **Government Grants**: Non-dilutive capital (no equity given away), e.g., **BIRAC BIG grant (₹50 Lakhs)** for biotech, **NIDHI-PRAYAS** for tech prototypes.
